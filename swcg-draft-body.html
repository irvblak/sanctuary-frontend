How to use this draft

The first four sections explain ownership, responsibility and the main systems. The middle sections describe ordinary work and technical changes. The final sections are practical checklists for training and eventual handover. Square brackets identify details that the Panel must decide or complete.

1 Purpose and guiding principles

The Sanctuary website provides residents with a single, accessible place for community information, events, private member services, Residents Association material and useful Club records. This guide explains how the website is governed, operated, protected and passed safely from one group of volunteers to the next.

The main conclusion

The website must not depend upon one individual. Subject to proper member approval, the Representative Panel should hold collective custody on behalf of the Sanctuary community, while suitably capable people carry out technical and administrative work under its authority.

Guiding principles

  • The website exists to serve residents and support the purposes of the Sanctuary Residents’ Association and Club.

  • The Panel decides policy. Technical access does not confer authority to make community decisions.

  • Each administrator uses an individual account. Shared passwords are avoided.

  • Access is limited to what each person needs for their work.

  • Personal information is accessed only for an authorised task and is never used for another purpose.

  • Changes are tested before publication and important actions leave an appropriate audit trail.

  • At least two trained people must be able to maintain essential services and recover access.

  • Instructions should be understandable by a willing successor who did not build the system.

What this guide does not contain

This guide does not contain passwords, recovery codes, private PINs, secret application keys, exported membership lists or copies of personal records. It also does not replace the Constitution, privacy notice or decisions properly made by members or the Panel.

2 Governance and responsibilities

Proposed governance position

The incumbent Panel is recommended to become collective Website Custodian on behalf of residents. This proposal should be confirmed through a properly notified member decision. Until then, the website may be used to support the Association’s existing constitutional purposes, while permanent custody remains a proposal rather than an accomplished fact.

Role Main responsibility Authority and limits
Members Approve the permanent governance position and any constitutional change. Retain the democratic authority of the Association.
Representative Panel Proposed collective custodian; sets policy, appoints and removes authorised people, and ensures continuity. Does not need to perform technical work itself. Must act within the Constitution and member decisions.
Technical Adviser Explains options, develops and maintains the system, trains successors and reports material risks. Advises and implements authorised decisions; does not determine Association or Club policy.
Website Administrator Uses the protected backend administration system and performs authorised exceptional operations. Only a person with an enabled individual Website Administrator account. Destructive actions must be justified and audited.
Club Administrator Handles agreed day-to-day membership and Club administration, including routine PIN work. Does not automatically receive source-code, hosting or destructive technical authority.
Role Holder Creates or maintains content within an assigned role or activity. May act only within the scope granted through the Role Register.

Initial proposed appointments

  • Irving Blakey: willing to serve as Technical Adviser if appointed by the Panel.

  • Additional trained people: at least two should be identified and taught the system.

3 How the website fits together

The website is not one program in one place. Several services work together. Understanding their separate jobs is the key to maintaining the system safely.

Part What it does Where it operates
Website address Provides the memorable address residents enter. sanctuary-club.uk and its domain settings
Frontend Displays pages, menus, forms, calendars, notices, Library and Design Studio. Published by Netlify from source held in GitHub
Backend Checks permissions, processes logins and PINs, stores and retrieves protected data, events and publications. Flask application hosted by Render
Database and stores Hold member, resident, event, booking, publication and audit information. Protected backend storage on Render
Source repositories Hold the program files and change history. GitHub repositories
Development assistance Helps plan, write, review and test changes under human instruction. ChatGPT and Codex working with authorised repository access

A resident’s journey

1. The resident visits sanctuary-club.uk.

2. Netlify sends the visible page and its scripts to the resident’s browser.

3. When protected information or an action is required, the page contacts the Render backend.

4. The backend checks the submitted authority, performs the permitted operation and returns only the required result.

5. Protected data remains in backend storage rather than being published with the visible website.

4 The services we use

Service Purpose Custody requirement
GitHub Stores the frontend and backend source code, records changes and provides the source used for deployment. At least two named technical custodians should have individual access. Repository ownership and recovery must not depend on one person.
Netlify Builds and publishes the frontend and provides deployment status and basic visitor analytics. At least two named custodians should have access and know how to inspect or restore a deployment.
Render Runs the protected Flask backend and holds its persistent data storage. Access must be tightly limited. At least two authorised technical custodians need recovery capability.
Domain provider Controls sanctuary-club.uk and directs it to the published website. Renewal, ownership and recovery details must be recorded. The account must not be allowed to lapse.
ChatGPT and Codex Assist with design, documentation, code changes, investigation and testing under Irving’s or a successor’s direction. They are tools, not custodians or decision makers. Personal data must not be supplied or accessed without specific authority.
Email and WhatsApp Notify residents and distribute approved information outside the website. Contact lists are used only for authorised communication and are not included in this guide.
NatWest and FreeAgent Potential future finance services. Not presently integrated with the website. Any integration requires a separate decision and security review.

Account ownership record

For every service, the confidential account register should state the legal or practical account holder, authorised users, recovery method, renewal date, payment method, two-factor authentication position and last access review. It should reference—but never reproduce—the secure location of credentials and recovery codes.

5 How ordinary website work is done

Most useful work should be completed through the website itself. It should not require GitHub access or alteration of program code.

Task Normal route Who performs it

Create or amend an event Host Area and Your Design Studio Authorised Event Host
Manage bookings Host State of Play and member booking pages Host, member or authorised administrator as appropriate
Create Club or activity material Your Design Studio Assigned Role Holder
Publish permanent material Your Design Studio to the appropriate Library section Assigned Role Holder; editorial approval where required

When code is not required

New events, notices, role assignments, publications and ordinary membership changes are data or content. They should use the appropriate website controls. Code is required only when the behaviour, structure, security or appearance of the system itself must change.

6 How a technical change is made

The following procedure reflects the current working method. It separates discussion, implementation, publication and verification so that an unfinished experiment does not quietly become the live system.

Step Action
1 Define the need Describe the problem or desired outcome in ordinary language. Identify who is affected and whether personal data, finance or permissions are involved.
2 Agree the authority Confirm whether the change is routine technical maintenance, a Panel policy matter or something requiring members’ approval.
3 Inspect the current system Review the relevant live behaviour and current source files before altering anything.
4 Prepare the change Edit the smallest appropriate set of frontend or backend files. Preserve unrelated work.
5 Test safely Run syntax and structural checks, then test the affected user journey without using genuine personal data unless specifically authorised.
6 Review the result Explain what changed, what remains unchanged and any limitation or risk.
7 Authorise publication Irving’s present instruction is the explicit phrase Update GitHub. A successor procedure should require similarly unambiguous authority.
8 Publish Commit the authorised files to the correct GitHub repository. Netlify or Render then deploys the relevant part.
9 Verify live Check the deployment status and repeat the important user journey on sanctuary-club.uk.
10 Record and hand over Record the date, purpose, responsible person, files or service affected, test result and any follow-up work.

Change classifications

Class Example Approval
Routine Correcting a broken link or explanatory wording without changing policy. Technical Adviser within agreed authority.
Operational Adding an approved activity, role route or administrative facility. Relevant Panel or Club authority, then technical implementation.
Sensitive Permissions, personal data, membership deletion, finance or account recovery. Explicit authorised decision, careful testing and audit record.
Governance Ownership, custody, voting, official communications or constitutional responsibility. Proper member or Panel decision as required by the Constitution.

7 Publishing and role holder work

The Role Register identifies who may act in each capacity. A person may hold more than one role. When entering Your Design Studio, the person selects the relevant hat; that choice determines the authorised route and destination.

Current publishing principle

  • Dated events are created for Calendar, Events List and their linked Notice.

  • Standing or lasting material is published to the appropriate Library or School section.

  • Writer and Artist work follows its editorial route before publication where required.

  • The creator’s name, membership reference and publication date provide provenance.

  • Work in progress remains private until publication.

Publishing checklist

  • Confirm that the author is wearing the correct role hat.

  • Check the intended audience and whether the material is public, members-only or Residents Association material.

  • Check names, dates, links, contact information and booking arrangements.

  • Preview the completed item.

  • Publish once to its authoritative destination; avoid unmanaged duplicate copies.

  • Open the published item as a member would and confirm that onward links work.

8 Membership administration and personal data

Membership administration is deliberately separated from technical custody. A household PIN permits that household to manage its own information. It does not confer administrator authority. Administrator access is used only for an authorised task.

Operation Authority Safeguard
View or amend household information Household using its private PIN. The PIN is not displayed to administrators.
Reset a forgotten PIN Club Administrator after independently confirming the request. Reset returns the household to temporary Starter PIN 2468; the member then chooses a new private PIN.
Clear Your Information Club Administrator only when properly authorised. All YI data is cleared or nothing is cleared; no partial selective removal.
Delete a membership Website Administrator authenticated through the protected Website Administrator account system. Permanent confirmation, removal of linked identity records and appropriate audit trail.
Export email addresses Authorised administrator for an approved communication. Use the minimum list required; do not retain unnecessary local copies.

Personal data rules

  • Never copy live member data into test files, development discussions or training examples unless specifically authorised and necessary.

  • Use invented membership references and contact details for testing.

  • Do not send or publish a membership list merely because technical access makes it possible.

  • Do not inspect personal records out of curiosity or for a purpose unrelated to the authorised task.

  • Delete temporary exports as soon as the authorised task is complete.

  • Report suspected disclosure, loss or unauthorised access immediately to the Panel and the person responsible for data protection.

9 Security and access

Individual accounts

GitHub, Netlify, Render and other important services should use individual named accounts where the service permits. This allows access to be removed without changing everyone’s credentials and makes responsibility clearer.

Minimum safeguards

  • Use a unique strong password for every service.

  • Enable two-factor authentication wherever available.

  • Keep recovery codes offline in an agreed secure location.

  • Give source-code and hosting access only to trained people who need it.

  • Review access at least every six months and whenever a role holder changes.

  • Disable access promptly when a person leaves the role.

  • Never send passwords, secret keys or complete recovery details through ordinary email or WhatsApp.

  • Do not place secrets in GitHub files, website pages, manuals or screenshots.

Separation of authority

Website Administrator status is separate from a Club Administrator role. A Club role must not silently acquire access to source code, hosting or destructive technical operations. Likewise, technical access does not entitle its holder to alter Club or Association policy.

10 Testing and deployment

Frontend deployment

A frontend change normally moves from the frontend GitHub repository to Netlify, which builds and publishes it at sanctuary-club.uk. A successful GitHub update is not by itself proof that the live deployment succeeded.

Backend deployment

A backend change moves from the private backend GitHub repository to Render. Render starts or updates the Flask service. Backend changes require particular care because they may affect authentication, permissions, stored data and every page that calls the service.

Minimum live test

  • Confirm Netlify or Render reports a successful deployment.

  • Open the live website in a fresh browser session or private window.

  • Test the exact route that was changed.

  • Test one nearby route that could reasonably have been affected.

  • Check desktop and narrow-screen presentation when appearance changed.

  • Confirm that protected pages still reject unauthorised access.

  • Check that Logout works where relevant.

  • Record the result and any limitation.

Safe test data

Use clearly identified test households and invented contact information. Remove test records when they are no longer needed. Never delete a real membership merely to test the deletion facility.

Visitor information

Netlify analytics may be used to view aggregate page visits and popular pages. The purpose is to understand whether the website is being used, not to identify or monitor individual residents. Any future analytics change should be checked against the published privacy notice.

12 Faults and recovery

First response

1. Record what the user attempted, the page address, the wording of any error and the approximate time.

2. Decide whether the fault affects one device, one page, all members or the whole service.

3. Check whether Netlify and Render report a failed deployment or service interruption.

4. Avoid repeated speculative changes while the cause is unknown.

5. If personal data or unauthorised access may be involved, stop ordinary investigation and notify the appropriate Panel contact immediately.

Recovery order

Problem First recovery action
New frontend change broke a page Identify the last known good GitHub version and redeploy or revert only the affected authorised change.
Backend deployment failed Read the Render deployment log, correct the specific error and redeploy. Do not experiment against live member data.
Domain no longer opens the site Check renewal and DNS settings before changing application code.
Administrator cannot sign in Check account status, token expiry and service availability. Use the agreed account-recovery route.
Data appears missing Do not recreate or overwrite records immediately. Preserve evidence, check the data store and seek a second technical review.
Suspected security incident Restrict access where safe, preserve logs, inform the Panel and follow the agreed data-incident procedure.

No destructive recovery by guesswork

Do not delete databases, repositories, accounts or broad groups of files in an attempt to restore service. Establish the exact target, preserve a recoverable copy where possible and obtain a second check before any exceptional destructive action.

13 Training and succession

The immediate continuity objective is to train at least two additional residents. Training should use the real system but avoid exposing personal data unnecessarily.

Session Outcome
1 Orientation Explain the website’s purpose, governance, frontend, backend, repositories and external services.
2 Member journey Use the live site as an ordinary resident, Role Holder, Club Administrator and Website Administrator.
3 Content work Create a harmless test item, preview it, publish it to the correct destination and remove it.
4 Source and deployment Locate both repositories, review a small change, observe a deployment and verify it live.
5 Security and recovery Practise account-recovery procedures without disclosing permanent secrets; review incident and rollback steps.
6 Supervised operation The trainee completes an agreed routine change and documents the result while the Technical Adviser observes.

Competence sign-off

The Panel should record which responsibilities each trained person can perform independently. Nobody needs access to every service merely to be useful. Full technical custody should be granted only when the person understands deployment, security, recovery and the limits of the role.

14 Handover checklist

When the Technical Adviser is unexpectedly unavailable

The Panel should contact the other named technical custodians, consult this guide and the confidential account register, and avoid unnecessary changes until control of GitHub, Netlify, Render and the domain has been confirmed. Routine content work may continue through the website if the system remains healthy.
